Where do 9,000 cruise passengers go on a Saturday?

Southampton turns around up to four cruise ships a day. Each one sends a few thousand people on foot through Dock Gates 4, 8 and 10 towards the station, the ferries, the cafés and the shops. Gangway models every walker as an agent on the city's street graph, so each café, pub and shop cluster gets a forecast of how many people will come through the door, hour by hour, and how many staff that needs.

Six simulations below show the same agents from different angles. The routing and the choice of where to go next are placeholders that the reinforcement-learning backend replaces.

How the backend plugs in

Every simulation asks one object where a passenger goes next: policy.decide(agent, context). Today that is a gravity model (bigger, closer, emptier venues win). The RL policy from src/sotonship26/rl replaces it, either live or as a recorded trace that Policy Lens can load and replay.

The street graph is hand-placed for now. scripts/export_frontend_graph.py rebuilds it from OpenStreetMap with the same node IDs, so nothing in the frontend changes. Design notes for each simulation are in docs/simulations/.
