How this forecast is made

  1. The network is Southampton's. one-way road links from OpenStreetMap, with lanes and speed limits, from motorways down to unclassified roads.
  2. Zones and gateways come from geography. zones built from OSM places. Each zone's homes, jobs and shops come from nearby residential, commercial, industrial, port and retail land. county gateways sit where the M27, M3, A35, A33 and other roads cross into the area.
  3. General traffic is generated, not assumed. Commuters from the county to the area's jobs and back, residents working outside, trips between zones (more between big, close zones), through traffic between gateways and county visitors to the shops. Each has its own pattern over the day, different at weekends. The school run adds drop-off and pick-up peaks on term-time weekdays only (assumed half-term 26–30 Oct). Match days add fans to St Mary's.
  4. Counted roads use the counts. On Portswood Road, Highfield Lane and Bevois Hill, measured peak speeds of 16–20 mph on 30 mph roads show they are at their limit, so the counted peak-hour flow sets their capacity. Local traffic the zones miss is added so flows match the counts, plus for university term, because the counts were taken in July.
  5. Cruise traffic is added per ship. Car drop-offs, park and cruise, taxis and coaches, timed by disembarkation and boarding and sent to the terminal's nearest dock gate.
  6. Routes are allocated dynamically. Every 15 minutes each of the origin–destination flows splits over up to three real routes and shifts towards the quickest given everyone's congestion. Traffic that cannot get through a full road waits and joins the next 15 minutes, so queues build and clear.
  7. Many sampled days. Ship delays, loads, travel choices and background traffic vary across samples. A road's chance of being at risk is the share of samples where it goes above 85% of capacity.

Backtest: not run yet

The demo has no observed traffic, so it cannot show accuracy. The product replaces the invented totals with Census 2021 travel-to-work flows and DfT counts, learns the cruise travel assumptions from 15-minute counts on past cruise days (lead candidate: National Highways WebTRIS on the M271), and tests on later days it has not seen.

It reports average error, Brier score and how often observations fall inside the 80% range, against three baselines: the last comparable cruise day, a model using passenger numbers only, and no cruise effect.

Not modelled yet

  • Queues blocking junctions upstream (queues wait at the bottleneck only)
  • School locations: OpenStreetMap was overloaded, so school trips follow where people live for now
  • Signal timings at individual junctions
  • Incidents, breakdowns and weather
  • Buses, rail and people switching mode when roads are busy
  • Traffic inside the port
  • County traffic from real origin–destination data (some bottlenecks, such as Itchen Bridge and Redbridge, run over capacity even without ships)

Only roads with sensors can be checked against reality. Every other road on this page is a model output.
