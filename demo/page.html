How this forecast is made

  1. Vehicles from the schedule. Each turnaround is split into car drop-offs and pick-ups, park and cruise, taxis and coaches. Their timing follows the disembarkation and boarding curves, and each goes to its terminal's dock gate. A coach counts as two cars.
  2. Background traffic. Weekday, Saturday and Sunday profiles on every road, raised around kick-off on match days. The product takes these from DfT traffic counts.
  3. Route choice. Traffic is split into packets of 20 vehicles. Each packet picks the quickest of up to four sensible routes; roads that fill up become slower until traffic spreads out.
  4. Many sampled days. versions of each day vary ship delays, how full ships are, how people travel and background traffic. A road's chance of being at risk is the share of those days where it goes above 85% of capacity in that 15 minutes.

Backtest: not run yet

The demo has no observed traffic, so it cannot show accuracy. The product learns the travel assumptions below from 15-minute traffic counts on past cruise days (lead candidate: National Highways WebTRIS on the M271), then tests on later days it has not seen.

It reports average error, Brier score and how often observations fall inside the 80% range, against three baselines: the last comparable cruise day, a model using passenger numbers only, and no cruise effect.

Assumptions in this demo

All invented. In the product these are learned from observed traffic, with ranges.

Not modelled

  • Incidents, breakdowns and weather
  • Queues backing up into the next 15 minutes
  • Signal timings at individual junctions
  • Traffic inside the port
  • Events that are not in the feeds

Only roads with sensors can be checked against reality. Every other road on this page is a model output.
