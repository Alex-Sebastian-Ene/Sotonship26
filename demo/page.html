    How the forecast works

    A road-network model run many times per date. The cruise effect is learned from past counts and tested on unseen days.

    Cruise assumptions learned from the M271

    Method, step by step
    1. The network is Southampton's. one-way road links from OpenStreetMap, with lanes and speed limits, from motorways down to unclassified roads.
    2. Zones and gateways come from geography. zones built from OpenStreetMap places. Each zone's homes, jobs and shops come from nearby residential, commercial, industrial, port and retail land. county gateways sit where the M27, M3, A35, A33 and other roads cross into the area. Where cutting the map to the area broke the M27 and M3, and where OpenStreetMap lacks the M3 to M27 eastbound link, the model joins them up.
    3. Everyday traffic is generated from data. Commuters from the county and back, residents working outside, trips between zones (more between big, close zones), through traffic, county visitors to the shops, and lorries to and from the port (fitted to DfT lorry counts on the M271). Each has its own pattern over the day, different at weekends. The school run adds drop-off and pick-up peaks on term-time weekdays (city council half-term 26–30 Oct). Match days add fans to St Mary's.
    4. Counted roads use the counts. On Portswood Road, Highfield Lane and Bevois Hill, measured peak speeds of 16–20 mph on 30 mph roads show they are at their limit, so the counted peak-hour flow sets their capacity. Local traffic the zones miss is added so flows match the counts, plus for university term, because the counts were taken in July.
    5. Cruise traffic is added ship by ship. Car drop-offs, park and cruise, taxis and coaches, timed by disembarkation and boarding and sent to the terminal's nearest dock gate.
    6. Drivers choose routes as roads fill. Every 15 minutes each of the origin–destination flows splits over up to three real routes and shifts towards the quickest. Traffic that cannot get through a full road waits for the next 15 minutes, so queues build and clear.
    7. Days vary. Drivers misjudge speeds, and everyone on a road shares the error. Everyday traffic swings by day, by kind of trip, by area and through the day. Passengers on one ship share its delay and travel mix. Wet days and incidents happen at their usual rates, and dates further ahead get wider ranges.
    8. Many simulated days. Each date is simulated times. A road's chance of congestion is the share of those days on which it goes over 85% of its capacity. Each simulated day is also run without the ships, so the ships' effect is measured on the same day.
    Trips per day What loads the network

    Scaled . Commuters from the county and residents working outside are Census 2021 car drivers crossing the edge of the area (). Through traffic and other county trips come from DfT counts on the roads into the area. Port freight is in car-equivalents (a lorry counts as 2.5 cars on a busy road), fitted to DfT lorry counts. Visitors, trips within the area and the school run are invented totals; trips within the area are fitted to council counts, and everything crossing the edge of the area to DfT counts, because census day fell in lockdown.     Checked against council counts Portswood, July 2024

    Trips within the area are scaled so that, summed over four counting sites around Portswood, modelled weekday flows match what was counted. Saturday is a check, not fitted. Single roads are less accurate, because each zone loads the network at one point. Source: .

    The same report gives bus run times on Portswood Road (Table 3-5): in July 2024 services ran 0:24 to 4:22 minutes behind timetable in the peaks, and a quarter of Bluestar 2 southbound buses were 5 or more minutes late. Modelled delays on Portswood Road should sit in that range out of term and above it in term.

    Checked against DfT counts Main roads into the area

    County commuters, visitors and through traffic are scaled together so that modelled average daily two-way flows match the DfT annual average daily flow (all motor vehicles) at these count points. Modelled flows are 05:00–20:00 on a weekday, Saturday and Sunday, weighted 5:1:1 and grossed up to 24 hours ( of traffic assumed between 05:00 and 20:00). Estimated points are DfT figures factored from an older count and count half in the fit. Itchen Bridge is tolled and the model has no tolls, so some of its traffic sits on Northam Bridge instead; both are checks, not fitted. Modelled port lorries count once here, not as 2.5 cars.     Port freight Lorries checked against DfT counts

    Lorries run between the county gateways (shared by each gateway road's DfT lorry count) and the port: . Their number is scaled so that the M271 between the A35 and junction 1, which leads only to the Western Docks and Redbridge, carries of its DfT lorry count (assumed port share). The other points also carry lorries that are not going to the port, so modelled port lorries should sit below their counts. Weekend days run at of a weekday, and lorries follow the hour-by-hour pattern of articulated lorries leaving the docks on the M271 (National Highways WebTRIS site 19974, 410 complete days, Oct 2024 to Jun 2026). The port's own gate counts would replace these assumptions. Source: .
